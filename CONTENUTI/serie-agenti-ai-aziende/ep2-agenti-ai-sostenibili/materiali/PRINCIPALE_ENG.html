How to build sustainable AI agents for businesses

System explainer: the review analyzer
What this document is. Supporting material to understand and explain the system while I record: how the review analyzer is built and — above all — why it costs so little, which is the point of the video. It is not a script to read out: it's the mental model + the charts to keep in front of me (and to show on screen).
In one sentence

What we build

A program that reads hundreds of customer reviews, pulls out average ratings, recurring topics and the cases that need urgent attention — spending a few cents instead of euros.

The trick isn't using the most powerful AI for everything. It's using the right model in the right place, and handing a plain script everything that doesn't need intelligence. Hence the keyword of the video: sustainable = cheap enough to keep running every day, for real.

How it's built

System architecture

Reviews come in from the left and pass through three layers. Most follow the cheap path; only the few delicate cases climb to the expensive layer.

Cheap model (volume) Script — zero cost Smart model (few cases) Delivered result
Piece by piece

How it works, layer by layer

1

The cheap model — the "fast intern" low cost

Model anthropic/claude-haiku-4-5. Reads every review and fills in a fixed-field form (structured output): rating 1–5, topic, tone, and a serve_revisione flag. The form forces a short answer → less text produced → less spend. This is the volume work: lots of it, repetitive, a perfect fit for a model that costs a fraction.

2

The script — the "calculator" €0

Plain Python code, runs on your PC. It computes averages, counts topics, sorts — and makes the most important decision in the system: which few cases to send to the expensive model. It does so with a precise rule and a fixed cap (MAX_CASI_SMART): this is the handbrake on costs. No AI here: calculations don't need intelligence, so they cost nothing.

3

The smart model — the "senior" high cost, few cases

Model anthropic/claude-opus-4-8, with reasoning on (OpenRouter's reasoning parameter). It steps in only on the delicate cases selected by the script: it assesses them, assigns a priority and drafts a reply. It costs more per case, but you only pay it on very few cases → the total stays light.

The point of the video

Why it costs little

Two ideas do all the work: (1) the volume is handled by the cheap model, not the powerful one; (2) a big chunk of the "work" (averages, counts, routing) isn't AI at all — it's a script, and a script costs zero. Only the tip of the iceberg is left for the expensive model.

The real numbers

What the two models cost

Prices are counted in tokens — small pieces of text, roughly a word each. You pay both for what the model reads (input) and what it writes (output). Prices per 1 million tokens:

ModelRole in the systemInputOutput
anthropic/claude-haiku-4-5Layer 1 — frames all reviews$1$5
anthropic/claude-opus-4-8Layer 3 — validates the few delicate cases$5$25
Python scriptLayer 2 — averages, counts, selection€0€0

For the same work, the powerful model costs 5 times more than the cheap one. That's why it pays to give it only the tip of the work — and keep the volume on the cheap model and the script. All models go through OpenRouter: one key for all models, and switching model is a single line (here we use Anthropic).

Honesty

What the system is NOT

Sustainable doesn't mean free. Every word the AI reads or writes is paid for: the point is that the bill is low enough to hold up over time.

The cheap model occasionally gets a review wrong. That's why the script acts as its safety net (it checks and counts) and the thorny cases pass under the smart model's eye anyway.

A human decides on customers. The smart model's draft reply is a starting point, not the final word.

The folder you hand over

What's in the materials

It's the materiali/ folder the viewer downloads and that you zip up at the end of the video. A handful of files, each with a precise role. Here's how it's laid out and how the pieces talk to each other.

FileWhat it is / what it's for
analizza_recensioni.pyThe program itself: the three layers (cheap model → script → smart model) and the final bill. It's the heart of the system.
recensioni.csvThe input data: the sample reviews (columns autore, testo). The viewer swaps in their own.
.env.exampleThe template for the credentials file. You copy it to .env and paste in the OPENROUTER_API_KEY.
requirements.txtThe list of packages to install (openai, python-dotenv) with pip install -r.
.gitignoreThe safety net for Git: it excludes .env, report.md and __pycache__, so credentials never end up online.
README.mdThe step-by-step guide for the public: how to switch the system on from scratch.
DISCLAIMER.mdThe note on key safety (don't share it, revoke it if it ends up online).
report.mdNot there at the start: the program creates it when it runs. It's the readable result (averages, topics, delicate cases with the draft reply).

In one line: recensioni.csv (input) + .env (the key) go into analizza_recensioni.py, which produces report.md (output) and prints the bill. README and DISCLAIMER are the instructions; .env.example, .gitignore and requirements.txt are there to get everything running safely.

Try it — operational part

How to test the system (with the course materials)

Everything you need is in the materiali/ folder. This is the exact sequence to switch it on and verify, with your own eyes, that the three layers really do their job.

  1. Open the materiali folder in VS Code and open the integrated terminal (Terminal → New Terminal).
  2. Install the dependencies: pip install -r requirements.txt
  3. Set the key: copy .env.example to .env and paste your OPENROUTER_API_KEY.
  4. Run the system: python analizza_recensioni.py

Proof that each layer did its part:
  • Open report.md: the delicate reviews (the lawyer threat, the refund, the overbooking) are in the "Casi delicati" section with a priority and a draft reply → the smart model stepped in only there.
  • The dozens of normal reviews did NOT go to Opus: they only appear in the counts and the average → the routing works.
  • The "THE BILL" box shows a few cents, several times less than "all-Opus" → it's sustainable.

Two extra checks (optional):

• The handbrake on costs. In analizza_recensioni.py change MAX_CASI_SMART = 5 to 2, save and re-run: at most 2 cases go to Opus and the bill drops even further. You've felt the spending cap first-hand.

• With your own data. Replace the contents of recensioni.csv with your own reviews (same two columns autore,testo) and re-run. Same system, your numbers.

How much does the test cost? One pass over the ~30 sample reviews costs a few cents. For an even cheaper test, trim recensioni.csv to 5 rows: the procedure is identical, the bill is a fraction.
