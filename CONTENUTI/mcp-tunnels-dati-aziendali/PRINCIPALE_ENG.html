STEP 1

What an MCP is — the Bluetooth of AI

What to show on screen

Whiteboard (digital on a tablet is fine). Draw live:

  • Left: stick figure "Claude" with bubble "I have a question about the products"
  • Middle: a large box labeled "MCP" — above it write in parentheses "(the intercom)"
  • Right: a "DATABASE" box with stylized tables inside
  • Two-way arrow Claude <-> MCP <-> DATABASE
  • Below the scene, a wall labeled "FIREWALL" and the MCP passes through it without breaking it — underline that point
What to say (verbatim)

"Now something that sounds boring, but stay with me for thirty seconds because this changes everything. In the past two years the big names in AI — Anthropic, OpenAI, Microsoft, Google — agreed on a common language with which AIs talk to business apps. It's called MCP. Translation: it's the Bluetooth of AI. You know Bluetooth — when it was introduced, suddenly every pair of headphones talked to every phone, without you installing software."

"The same thing is happening here. Today any new business app — Supabase, HubSpot, Notion, Google Drive — already ships with the Bluetooth of AI built in. You don't write code, you don't open ports, you don't configure proxies. You click a switch — called a 'connector' — and Claude sees the system."

"The analogy for the security part: think of it as the apartment building intercom. Claude is the delivery guy. From the landing he says 'I need the list of out-of-stock products'. You answer through the intercom. He doesn't enter the apartment, doesn't open the cupboards, doesn't see the shelves — he only sees what the intercom — the MCP — decides to answer. Safe by default."

"A new feature for people who have physical databases in their office: it's called MCP Tunnel, it dropped on May nineteenth, four days ago. If your software runs on a real server inside your business, not in the cloud, this feature opens a temporary channel between Claude and your server, lasts only the conversation, then closes. No IT to disturb. No VPN. Setup is a touch more technical, an hour or two of help from your tech person, and then it just runs."

STEP 5

The interesting part — Claude finds the real problems

What to show on screen

New chat on claude.ai. Fire the prompts in order, showing the responses and zooming where the problem emerges. Suggested pacing:

  • Prompt 1 (warm-up): "What tables do I have in the connected database?" — show how Claude grasps the structure
  • Prompt 2 (bestsellers): "Which 5 wines sold the most in the last 30 days?" — standard response, ordered table
  • Prompt 3 (KEY POINT): "Do I have products about to run out without me noticing? Consider 'at risk' products whose stock will cover less than 21 days of sales." — Claude finds Chianti, Vermentino, Lambrusco. **Zoom on the table**.
  • Prompt 4 (cashier error): "Check if there are products where I'm selling below cost." — Claude finds Barolo and Champagne. **Pause, let the viewer digest**.
  • Prompt 5 (out-of-stock still visible): "Are there products that are out of stock but still orderable online?" — Claude finds Prosecco, Pinot Grigio, Moscato. Explains the site is taking orders it cannot fulfill.
  • Prompt 6 (summary): "Generate a 'warehouse health' dashboard. The 5 things to fix this week, by economic impact." — Claude pulls everything together. **Show the final output as the "ok I have the week's plan" moment**.
What to say (verbatim)

"I open a new chat. First prompt, easy: 'What tables do I have in the connected database?' Claude looks, understands, explains I have a product catalog and a sales history. It already knows it's a wine shop. I don't have to tell it."

"Second prompt, something useful: 'Which five wines sold the most in the last thirty days?' It pulls the ranking with quantity, orders, revenue. Ready to drop into a presentation. Nothing earth-shattering — a well-built Excel sheet does this too."

"Third prompt — here things get interesting. 'Do I have products about to run out without me noticing? Consider at-risk those whose stock will cover less than twenty-one days of sales.' Claude analyzes sales and stock, does the math, comes back with Chianti, Vermentino, Lambrusco. Below threshold, heavy sales. Translated: if I don't reorder this week, in ten days the site says 'out of stock' and I lose live revenue. This, from an Excel sheet, you don't pull out easily."

"Fourth prompt — and here I take a breath because this stuff hurts: 'Check if there are products where I'm selling below cost.' Claude checks. And gives me two names: Barolo, which I sell at twenty-five but costs thirty-two. Champagne, I sell at fifty-eight, costs sixty-five. I'm losing money on every bottle. Nobody noticed. Probably typos in the last price list update. Claude also calculates how much I've already lost in the last ninety days — four hundred and twenty euros on Barolo, one forty on Champagne. Small number for a big chain, number that makes the difference for a small wine shop."

"Fifth prompt: 'Are there products that are out of stock but still orderable online?' It finds Prosecco, Pinot Grigio, Moscato d'Asti. Three wines my site is promising to clients without being able to deliver. Complaints incoming. One-star reviews. Translated into action: this afternoon I hide the three products from the online listing until the new shipment arrives."

"Sixth prompt — the one I take home from the video: 'Generate a warehouse health dashboard. The five things to fix this week, by economic impact.' Claude pulls together everything it found up to here, prioritizes by euros, gives me a five-point operational plan. My Monday morning is done. Five minutes, dashboard ready, decisions to make. Without me writing a single query, without me opening Excel."

In this step the directing matters a lot. For each prompt: type slowly, let the viewer read the question, give three seconds of "watching Claude think" before the response. Don't rush — the slowness here is what makes the power of the tool feel real.
STEP 6

Bonus — Claude writes the emails to the producers

What to show on screen

Same chat. Show:

  • Prompt: "For each product running low from prompt 3, write a draft email to the producer to reorder stock. Save it as a Gmail draft. Don't send."
  • Claude works, show the 3 generated emails (cordial but professional tones, each personalized for the producer)
  • Open Gmail, go to Drafts, show that the 3 drafts are actually there waiting for review

NB: this step needs Google Workspace connected as a connector — do that before recording, so the request works on the first try.

What to say (verbatim)

"One thing I like about Claude when you put it on multiple tools at once: you can ask it not just to find the problem, but to start solving it. Let's see. Prompt: 'For each product running low from prompt three, write a draft email to the producer to reorder stock. Save it as a Gmail draft.'"

"Claude takes the three wines — Chianti, Vermentino, Lambrusco — and writes three separate emails, each addressed to the right producer, each with the right tone. Saves them in my Gmail drafts folder. And here I want to stress one thing: it doesn't send them, it puts them as drafts. You go, you reread them, optionally fix something, and YOU click Send. Never blind automation. Always your final review for things that go out."

"Result: a job that would have cost me twenty minutes a week — pulling contacts, writing each email, managing tone — done in thirty seconds. Small example, but if you replicate this on ten parts of your operation, the time you take back every week matters."
