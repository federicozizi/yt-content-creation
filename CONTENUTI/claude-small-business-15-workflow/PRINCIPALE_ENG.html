STEP 4

Monday morning with `/monday-brief`

What to show on screen

New chat in Cowork. Show:

  • You type /monday-brief and hit enter
  • Claude starts working — show the tool icons it's querying (Calendar, Gmail, HubSpot) with micro loading indicators
  • Final output: well-formatted markdown report in 4 sections
  • Zoom on the "Priorities of the week" section
  • Also show a customized variant with enriched prompt ("/monday-brief sales focus, informal Italian tone")
What to say (verbatim)

"First moment of my week: Monday, eight-thirty, coffee in hand. I open Cowork, new chat. Type slash-monday-brief. Hit enter."

"Claude starts working. You see here — it's reading the week's calendar, scanning unread emails from the last seven days, querying HubSpot for recent deals. In forty-to-sixty seconds it gives me a one-pager with four sections: priorities of the week, meetings with prep notes, client conversations waiting for response, and last week's numbers."

"What I take away: this morning, while I was having coffee, I already had the map of the week. No calendar open, no inbox open, no sticky notes. Five things to do, in order, with all the useful context."

"A note on personalization, because the first time you run it Claude asks you for some context — what you do, what your typical priorities are, what language you want the brief in. Answer once. The next weeks the brief comes out already tuned to how you work. I can also say on the fly: slash-monday-brief sales focus informal Italian tone. It recalibrates for me, and keeps the new tone for the next runs."

STEP 5

Wednesday afternoon — a new lead arrives, `/lead-qualifier`

What to show on screen

Scene setup:

  • Show HubSpot — a new contact just loaded (company name, business email, partially filled industry)
  • Go to Cowork, new chat
  • Type /lead-qualifier
  • Claude works: you see it searches the company website, reads it, possibly searches LinkedIn for the contact
  • Final output: 1-page sheet with industry, estimated size, indicative revenue, recent public signals, motivated MQL/SQL score
  • Zoom on the "Why this lead matters" part to read it well
What to say (verbatim)

"Second moment. Wednesday afternoon. Three-thirty. A new lead comes in from the contact form on my site — company name, business email. I have a call with this person Thursday at ten. Before the call I'd like to know who I have on the other side. Traditionally, this means thirty minutes of Google, LinkedIn, company website open in several tabs."

"I save the contact in HubSpot — name, email, last touch. Open Cowork, new chat. Type slash-lead-qualifier."

"Claude takes the most recent lead from HubSpot and does the enrichment work. Searches the company website, reads it. Estimates industry, size, revenue band from public signals — how many people on LinkedIn, how many regions they operate in, how long they've been around. Checks if the company has significant public presence — recent news, reviews, possible funding rounds. And calculates a motivated MQL slash SQL score — how interesting they are as a lead and why."

"Output: a one-page sheet I read in two minutes before the call. I know who I'm talking to, what stage the business is at, where to steer. Worth being honest about a limit: Claude works with public information. It's not an intelligence agency. On very small or very young businesses the output will be thin — that's not Claude's fault, the information isn't online to begin with."

STEP 6

Friday evening — week's wrap-up with `/invoice-chaser`

What to show on screen

Scene setup: HubSpot with 3 deals/clients in "unpaid invoice" status, different ages (15d, 45d, 80d). Prepare this data before recording.

  • Cowork, new chat, type /invoice-chaser
  • Claude scans, finds the 3 invoices, classifies by age
  • Generates 3 email drafts with different tones (polite, firm, escalation)
  • **Important**: open Gmail, go to Drafts, show that the 3 drafts are really there — not sent
  • Open one of the drafts (the "firm" one for 45 days) and show the full text
What to say (verbatim)

"Third moment. Friday, five-thirty in the afternoon. About to wrap up. There's one thing I'd like to have done this week and haven't: invoice chasing. Three clients overdue, different ages, different tones to use. Work that takes me forty minutes to do well, and that — if I'm tired — I push to next week. And then another. You know how it goes."

"Open Cowork. New chat. Type slash-invoice-chaser."

"Claude checks HubSpot — or QuickBooks if I use that for accounting — looking for overdue invoices. Finds the three situations: one overdue by fifteen days, one by forty-five, one by eighty days. And here it does the interesting thing: it classifies by age and generates three email drafts with completely different tones."

"For fifteen days it's a 'friendly reminder' — cordial, no pressure. For forty-five it becomes firm, and proposes a solution like an installment plan. For eighty days it's a polite but decisive escalation, mentioning a move to collections if not resolved. Three tones, three situations, calibrated."

"One thing I want to stress out loud: Claude does NOT send the emails. It saves them as drafts in the Gmail Drafts folder. See here, here they are — three drafts waiting. I open them one by one, reread, optionally edit a sentence, and I click Send. Never blind automation on communications going to clients. The rule is simple: Claude prepares, you confirm. The final call on what leaves your office stays yours."
